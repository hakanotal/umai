How it works

The model looks. The code counts.

A vision model is superb at recognising a plate and dreadful at arithmetic. So Umai splits the job in four: the model only ever returns what and how much, and every calorie figure is computed afterwards in ordinary code.

Use cases

One chat, eight jobs

Everything happens in the same conversation. There is no app to open and no dashboard you have to remember to visit.

Why it is built this way

Trustworthy beats impressive

It knows what it does not know

Every food carries a trust tier, from laboratory data down to a figure a model proposed. A provisional number is never quietly presented as fact.

Consistency over cleverness

An estimate that swings by a third between two photos of the same plate is useless. Umai is tuned for low run-to-run scatter, not for a flattering average.

It learns your kitchen

Tell it which cuisines you eat and a plate stops being "flatbread with a reddish topping" and becomes lahmacun — a name that actually matches a food row.

Fixes reach backwards

Correct a food once and every past meal and saved recipe that used it is corrected too. Your history gets more accurate the longer you use it.

Safety lives in the code

Targets never fall below your BMR or a hard floor, weight loss is capped at a sane weekly rate, and protein has a minimum. No conversation can talk it out of these.

It is your server

Umai runs on hardware you own — a Raspberry Pi is plenty. Your meals, weight and photos stay there, and /export and /delete_me do exactly what they say.

The whole surface

A dozen commands, six buttons

Joining is a short chat — timezone, sex, height, birth date, starting weight, goal rate and cuisines. After that you mostly just send photos.
